# CV and Cover Letter Guidelines

## Core principle
A CV and a cover letter serve two different purposes.
- **CV:** Demonstrates what you have done, what you can do, and what evidence supports your capabilities.
- **Cover letter:** Explains why you are applying, why your background is relevant, and what you could contribute to that particular organisation.
They should complement each other, not repeat each other.

## CV structure (9 sections, locked order)

| # | Section | Content | Priority |
|---|---------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Header | Name, headline, `Email:` / `LinkedIn:` / `GitHub:` / `Website:` / `Work permit:` rows | Essential |
| 2 | PROFESSIONAL PROFILE | Professional positioning and strongest evidence | Essential |
| 3 | CORE COMPETENCIES | Relevant professional capabilities, bold labels | Essential |
| 4 | TECHNICAL SKILLS | Technologies, tools, platforms, protocols, methodologies | Essential for technical roles |
| 5 | PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE | Employers, positions, dates, responsibilities, achievements (reverse chronological, 2-4 bullets per role) | Essential |
| 6 | EDUCATION | Degrees, institutions, dates, relevant thesis/research | Essential |
| 7 | CERTIFICATIONS | Canonical list: CompTIA Security+, CompTIA Network+, Linux Administration, Cisco Interconnecting Scaling Networks, Goethe-Zertifikat B2 | Essential |
| 8 | SELECTED PROJECTS | Personal/professional projects demonstrating practical skills | Optional - include only when page space allows; second-to-last |
| 9 | LANGUAGES | Languages and honest proficiency levels | Essential |

Do not reorder or merge sections. SELECTED PROJECTS is the only optional section. Work permit belongs in the header, not in a separate "Additional information" section.

### Effective section guidance

**Professional headline (header):** Avoid generic titles ("IT Professional"). Communicate professional positioning.
Example: "Senior Solutions Consultant | Technical Implementation | Network & Systems Operations"

**PROFESSIONAL PROFILE:** Professional introduction, not autobiography. Communicate: years/areas of experience, strongest capabilities, industries/environments, what distinguishes you. Keep concise.

**PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE:** Most important section. For each position: company + location, job title, dates, 2-4 achievement bullets with evidence. tripunkt GmbH is split into two entries (QA Engineer first, then Software Solutions Consultant).
- Weak: "Responsible for technical support" → Stronger: "Investigated complex enterprise software and integration issues across international customer environments."
- Weak: "Worked with engineering teams" → Stronger: "Coordinated Tier 3 escalations between customers, QA and engineering teams."
- Weak: "Performed software testing" → Stronger: "Coordinated four QA team members and approximately 100 test cases per release."
- Weak: "Improved processes" → Stronger: "Improved diagnostic and handover workflows, contributing to a reported ~30% reduction in MTTR."

**Technical skills:** Organise into categories, not one enormous keyword list. Only list technologies you can discuss confidently. Distinguish professional experience from academic knowledge and personal experimentation.

**Projects:** Valuable for transitioning into new technical domains. Include: name, purpose, technologies, what you implemented, technical challenges, current status.

## Cover letter structure (5 parts)

1. **Header:** Contact details, employer details, date, subject.
2. **Opening paragraph:** Identify the position, establish professional relevance. 2-3 sentences.
3. **Relevant experience:** Select 2-3 examples from professional history demonstrating suitability. Main body.
4. **Motivation and contribution:** Why this opportunity makes sense, what you bring. Connect experience to employer's needs.
5. **Closing:** Express interest in discussing, close professionally.

### What each paragraph achieves

| Paragraph | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Opening | Establish the position and your relevance |
| Experience | Demonstrate relevant professional achievements |
| Technical fit | Show how your skills align with the role |
| Motivation | Explain why the opportunity is a logical next step |
| Closing | Invite further discussion |

### What a cover letter should NOT contain
- Your entire employment history
- A copy of your CV in paragraph form
- A long list of technical skills
- Unsupported claims about being an expert
- Generic statements ("I am passionate about innovation")
- Excessive flattery about the company
- Personal circumstances that don't support your application
- Desperate language

## CV vs cover letter: practical difference

| Subject | CV | Cover letter |
|---------|-----|-------------|
| Six years at tripunkt | Employment history with dates and responsibilities | Briefly explains the professional experience gained |
| 600+ accounts | Quantifies customer and partner exposure | Explains ability to handle complex stakeholder environments |
| Tier 3 escalations | Documents technical troubleshooting experience | Demonstrates problem-solving approach |
| QA leadership | Shows team coordination and release validation | Explains ability to collaborate with engineering |
| Networking background | Lists relevant infrastructure experience | Connects that experience to a network/security position |
| Personal projects | Documents practical technical development | Mentions only projects relevant to the vacancy |

**The CV provides the evidence. The cover letter interprets that evidence in the context of the job.**

## Recommended lengths (German/European market)

| Document | Recommended length |
|----------|-------------------|
| Targeted CV | 2 pages (A4) |
| Master CV | 2 pages (A4) |
| Cover letter | 1 page (A4), 250-350 words |
| Technical project portfolio | Separate document or website |

## Professional application writing standard (baseline)

**CV**
- Two pages, A4.
- Achievement-driven professional experience.
- Technical capabilities supported by evidence.
- Clear professional positioning.
- ATS-readable formatting.
- Consistent, defensible metrics.
- Relevant keywords from the vacancy.

**Cover letter**
- One page, A4.
- 250-350 words.
- Written specifically for each vacancy.
- Two or three relevant pieces of evidence.
- Explicit connection between experience and job requirements.
- Genuine company-specific motivation.
- No generic introductions or repeated CV content.

**Writing quality**
- Natural professional language.
- No AI-generated corporate filler.
- No unsupported achievements.
- No exaggerated technical expertise.
- No redundant descriptions.
- Every sentence must serve a purpose.

## Practical considerations
- Use a clean, consistent layout.
- Use reverse chronological order for employment.
- Use readable fonts and consistent formatting.
- Avoid unnecessary graphics, progress bars and skill ratings.
- Keep the CV ATS-readable.
- Include a professional photograph only if appropriate; not mandatory in Germany.
- Submit PDF unless the employer requests another format.
- Use truthful, consistent employment dates and qualifications.
